Order it cut to length. Every supplier cuts to order and their ends are square. A hacksawn 1800 mm extrusion is out of square, and an out-of-square frame draws parallelograms that no software setting will fix.

Roughly 12.3 m of 2020 total — order 14 m for waste. Cross members are 1760 because they sit between the uprights, giving an outer envelope of exactly 1800.

Assemble flat on the floor, corner brackets and T-nuts, then measure both diagonals before final tightening — they must match within 2 mm. That single measurement is the difference between a machine that draws squares and one that draws rhombuses.

Pen change

Four markers sit in a rack above the drawing area, each in a printed puck. The carriage drives up, magnets grab a puck, it draws, it puts it back and takes another.

It needs no extra motor and no extra wiring — a pen change is just X/Y moves, so it costs nothing electrically. Hit play and watch one happen.

One unsolved detail

The rack has to grip a puck harder than the carriage magnets do (or it can never let go), yet let the carriage pull one out (or it can never pick up). Those two demands contradict each other. Real tool changers solve it with a lateral escape: the rack resists straight pull but not a sideways slide. That geometry is the one thing on this machine still to be designed.

Decided

Full size in one go · freestanding leaning frame · magnetic whiteboard with paper laid on by hand · rail levelled by hand against top stops at power-on · no homing cycle · two switches as hard limits only.
