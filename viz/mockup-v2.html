Order it cut to length. Every supplier cuts to order and their ends are square. A hacksawn 1800 mm extrusion is out of square, and an out-of-square frame draws parallelograms that no software setting will fix.

Roughly 12.3 m of 2020 total — order 14 m for waste. Cross members are 1760 because they sit between the uprights, giving an outer envelope of exactly 1800.

Assemble flat on the floor, corner brackets and T-nuts, then measure both diagonals before final tightening — they must match within 2 mm. That single measurement is the difference between a machine that draws squares and one that draws rhombuses.

Pen

One black Sharpie Fine Point, clamped in the spring-loaded shuttle. No pen changer, no rack, no magnets — that whole mechanism is cut.

Cutting it bought back the 130 mm rack strip, so the drawing area went from 1600×850 to 1600×980. A0 landscape now sits with 70 mm clearance top and bottom instead of 4.5 mm, which means paper magnets fit on all four edges and the clamp bar is no longer needed either.

Nothing is foreclosed. The changer was always a bolt-on to the bottom rail; the Cartesian architecture that makes it possible is unchanged. Add it next semester to a machine that already runs.

Still true with one pen

A Sharpie fades over a 2 hour continuous plot as the tip softens. Feed-hold (!), swap, cycle-start (~) — the spring absorbs the length difference so the tip returns to the same pressure. Budget a swap around 90 min, and test one pen against a long plot first so you know when it goes.

Decided

Full size in one go · freestanding leaning frame · magnetic whiteboard with paper laid on by hand · rail levelled by hand against top stops at power-on · no homing cycle · two switches as hard limits only · one black pen, no changer.
